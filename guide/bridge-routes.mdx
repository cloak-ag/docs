Funds from another network reach your Cloak balance through one route: **NEAR Intents (1Click)**. This page says what that route promises, what your browser verifies for itself, and what you are still trusting.

If you have not read [Bridging in from another chain](/guide/bridge) yet, start there; this page assumes it.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Networks it serves into Cloak | Base, Polygon, Sui |
| Signs the deposit address and the recipient | **Yes** |
| Checked in your browser before your wallet is asked to pay | **Yes** |
| Refunds an order it cannot fill | **Yes**, to the wallet you sent from |
| Deposit address | Fresh per order, with a deadline |
| Delivery status the app can ask for | Yes |
| Holds your funds between networks | **Yes.** See [The trust nobody can remove](#the-trust-nobody-can-remove) |

## Why the signature matters

Without one, a deposit address is a string a web page handed to your wallet. A substituted address and a real one look identical, and you would find out by having paid it.

The route signs a payload that contains **both** the deposit address and the Solana recipient. Your browser verifies that signature against the route's key before your wallet is asked to approve anything, and an option whose signature fails is not offered at all.

<Check>
A substituted deposit address is detectable **even if Cloak substituted it**. The check does not rely on Cloak being honest; it relies on arithmetic your browser runs.
</Check>

Two limits on that, because a guarantee stated without its edges is not a guarantee:

- **The signature covers amounts, the address and the recipient, not fee captions.** Fee figures sit outside the signed payload, so they carry no such proof. The app computes what it shows you from the signed amounts.
- **A compromised web page could lie about the result.** The verification runs in the browser, so a tampered bundle could claim a check passed. This is why [verified addresses](/guide/verified-addresses) matters: confirm you are on the real Cloak app first, and the check underneath is sound.

## You never handle the address

The app passes the verified address to your wallet itself. There is nothing to copy, and so nothing to paste wrong. What you approve is one transfer, for the amount on screen, on the network you chose.

## Refunds

An order the route cannot fill is refunded to **the wallet you sent from, on the network you sent from**. You do not supply a refund address; it is the sending wallet.

The app reports a refund when the route reports one. That report is the route's word, not a ledger: check the wallet to confirm.

Each order has a deadline, signed with the quote. The app asks your wallet to approve only while enough time is left, and otherwise sends nothing and asks you to start over.

## The trust nobody can remove

The route holds your funds between your transfer on the origin network and the payout on Solana. That is how every bridge that is not burn-and-mint works. Cloak did not introduce it, and no part of Cloak's design removes it.

It can be **bounded and disclosed**, which is what this page is for. The honest framing is never "trustless or not". It is *which trust, how bounded, and how visible*. Here:

- **In transit:** the route holds the funds.
- **At the address:** the funds are yours the moment they land. The receiving key is derived from your own key material and no server has ever held it.
- **In the pool:** the pool's guarantees take over, and the [security model](/guide/security) applies as it does to any other deposit.
