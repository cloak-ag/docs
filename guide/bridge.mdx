---
title: "Bridging in from another chain"
description: "Send from Base, Polygon or Sui and have it arrive as a private USDC balance on Solana, with no public Solana wallet of yours in the middle"
icon: "bridge"
---

Shielding starts with tokens you already hold on Solana. Bridging starts one chain earlier: you send from Base, Polygon or Sui, and it arrives as a **private USDC balance** on Solana, without ever sitting in a Solana wallet that anyone can tie to you.

That last part is the point. The ordinary way to do this is to bridge into your own Solana wallet and then shield, which publishes the one link you were trying to avoid: *this wallet received a cross-chain transfer, and then this wallet deposited into the pool.* Bridging into Cloak removes the middle step.

## Where you can bridge from

| Origin network | You can send | It arrives as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base | USDC, ETH | USDC in your private balance |
| Polygon | USDC | USDC in your private balance |
| Sui | USDC | USDC in your private balance |

Ethereum and Arbitrum appear in the list as **Coming soon**. They cannot be selected yet.

<Warning>
Send from the network you picked. USDC on Ethereum is not USDC on Base: if your funds are on a network marked Coming soon, move them yourself first, or wait. Picking Base while holding Ethereum USDC does not work.
</Warning>

## What actually happens

<Steps>
<Step title="You open Add money and choose From another network">
  Pick the network, the coin and the amount. Your Solana wallet stays connected, because that is how Cloak derives your keys. You also connect a wallet on the network you are sending from: a browser wallet for Base and Polygon, a Sui wallet for Sui.
</Step>

<Step title="Cloak shows you what will arrive">
  Before you approve anything, the sheet shows roughly how much USDC lands in your private balance, after the route's cut and the Solana network cost.
</Step>

<Step title="Your wallet asks you to approve one transfer">
  One transfer, on the network you are sending from. There is no deposit address to copy or paste: the app hands the address to your wallet itself, after checking the route's signature over it.
</Step>

<Step title="The route delivers USDC on Solana">
  It lands at a **one-time receiving address** derived from your own Cloak keys. The funds are already yours and nothing else can spend them, but for those moments they sit in the open, where the amount and the address are visible like any other Solana balance.
</Step>

<Step title="Cloak adds it to your private balance">
  The USDC moves from the one-time address into the pool. Your Solana wallet signs nothing for this and pays nothing: the Solana network cost comes out of the amount that arrived.

  <Check>
  What you end up with is USDC in your private balance, the same thing you would have had by shielding USDC you already held. It sends and withdraws the same way.
  </Check>
</Step>
</Steps>

At no point does a Solana wallet of yours hold the money. The only Solana address that touches it is single-use and derived from keys only you hold. It is derived, not stored, so there is nothing extra to back up: Cloak finds it again from your keys alone.

<Note>
The only thing your Solana wallet may sign is the Cloak sign-in message, and only if your private balance is not already unlocked. It does not sign the deposit and never appears on-chain in this flow.
</Note>

## The last step needs the app open

Adding the money to your private balance runs in your browser. If you close the sheet or the tab after your transfer and before that step, nothing is lost: what arrives waits at your one-time address, which only your keys control.

To finish it, connect the same Solana wallet, open **Add money**, and choose **From another network** again. Cloak finds what is waiting from your keys alone, shows it as *arrived and is waiting*, and adds it. This works on a device that has never seen the deposit. There is no time limit.

## What it costs

**Arriving carries no Cloak fee.** A bridged deposit is a deposit, and deposits never carry one. What comes off the amount is this:

| What | Who sets it | When you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The route's cut for moving it to Solana | The route. The quote is signed with a guaranteed minimum, and the sheet is computed from that minimum, not from a hoped-for figure. | Before you approve |
| The Solana network cost | A fixed USDC amount taken out of what arrives. You reach Solana holding no SOL, and putting tokens into the pool costs SOL, so that cost is covered for you and comes off the amount. It is not a Cloak fee. | Before you approve; the exact charge is set when the money is added |
| Cloak fee to arrive | **None.** | |
| Cloak fee to leave, later | The standard USDC exit fee, collected on-chain by the program. See [Fees](/guide/fees). | When you withdraw or send |

## Minimums

- For Base and Polygon the sheet suggests a minimum of about 3 USDC.
- The hard rule is that at least **1 USDC** must be left to add after the Solana network cost. That is the pool's own minimum deposit. If less would be left, the button reads *Too little would arrive* and nothing is sent.
- The route can set a temporary minimum of its own for a network. When it does, the sheet says so, with the figure.

## If the order cannot be completed

The route refunds an order it cannot fill **to the wallet you sent from, on the network you sent from**. The sheet tells you when the route reports a refund, with the amount, and that nothing was added to your private balance. Check that wallet to confirm.

An order also has a deadline. The app only asks your wallet to approve while there is enough time left; if the deadline is too close, it sends nothing and asks you to start over.

## If something is waiting and cannot be added

Three things can leave USDC at one of your one-time addresses, outside your private balance:

- **Too little arrived.** At or under the pool's minimum, it can never be added.
- **Someone else sent money to your receiving address.** Cloak only adds money that your own deposit explains. A stray amount of up to 1 USDC is discarded and your deposit goes through. A larger one stops the deposit: Cloak will not add money it cannot account for.
- **Adding it keeps failing** for another reason.

In each case the app offers **Move it to my wallet**. That sends everything at that address to your connected Solana wallet in one transfer, which your wallet approves and pays the network fee for.

<Note>
**Your wallet needs a little SOL for this.** The fee is paid in SOL by your wallet and is not taken from the money being moved. It is about 0.002 SOL the first time your wallet receives USDC, and much less after that. If your wallet is short, the app says how much it needs before asking you to sign, and nothing is sent.
</Note>

<Warning>
**Moving it to your wallet is a public transfer.** Anyone can see it, and it links that one-time address to your wallet. It does not go into your private balance; you can shield it from your wallet afterwards. It also moves *everything* at that address, including money that did not come from your deposit. The app says all of this on the confirm screen before your wallet is asked to sign.
</Warning>

The option appears only after an attempt to add the money has failed, or when the amount is too small to ever be added. While the money is being added normally, it is not offered.

## Screening

Before a deposit starts, Cloak screens the wallets involved. Before it adds what arrived, it screens every wallet that actually paid into your deposit. If a check fails, the app says the transfer cannot be completed with the connected wallets. If a check cannot be run, it says so and asks you to try again shortly. See [Compliance](/guide/compliance).

## What this hides, and what it doesn't

**Hidden:** the link between your one-time receiving address and everything you do afterwards. Once the funds are in the pool, a chain-watcher sees a deposit from an address with no history, and nothing that connects it to your wallet, your other deposits, or any payment you later make.

**Not hidden:** the origin leg. Your transfer on Base, Polygon or Sui is a public transaction on that network, and the route sees which wallet paid and which Solana address it delivered to. That is true of every bridge that is not burn-and-mint, and no Cloak-side change removes it. It is bounded, though: the route's knowledge stops at the one-time address.

**Visible briefly:** the window between arrival and the money being added, where the amount sits in the open at your one-time address.

**Given up if you use it:** *Move it to my wallet* publishes the link between that one-time address and your wallet.

<Tip>
The habits from [How Cloak works](/guide/how-it-works#getting-the-most-privacy) apply here too. Letting time pass between arriving and spending, and avoiding distinctive amounts, do more for your privacy than anything in the bridge itself.
</Tip>

## If your funds don't appear

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="1. Reopen Add money, From another network">
Connect the same Solana wallet. Anything that arrived while the app was closed shows as *arrived and is waiting* and is added from there. See [The last step needs the app open](#the-last-step-needs-the-app-open).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="2. Check the wallet you sent from">
If the route could not fill the order, it refunds to that wallet on the origin network. The sheet reports a refund when the route does.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="3. If the app says it could not be added">
Use *Try again now*. If it keeps failing, or the amount is under the minimum, *Move it to my wallet* is the way out. Read what it costs in privacy first, above.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="4. If none of that explains it">
Bring the hash of your origin-network transfer to the support bot, [@cloak_support_bot](https://t.me/cloak_support_bot). It is public and reveals nothing about your private balance.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
Cloak cannot reverse a transfer, and cannot recover funds a route never delivered.
</Warning>
